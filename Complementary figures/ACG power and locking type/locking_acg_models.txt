Locking-period autocorrelogram models
======================================

The statistics file and the figure title use the same name. Every lock
group of one model is written into that one statistics file. Set
models_to_plot to the model numbers whose figures should open, for
example [13 14 15]. The statistics are printed for every model.

Reported coefficients in models 1-11 are standardized ordinary-least-squares
coefficients unless a line below says otherwise. A row is removed before a
fit when a predictor or the outcome has a modified z-score above 3.5.
PPC is not a predictor of LFP delta power, because the PPC estimate uses
that power.

The default autocorrelogram smooth is smooth_half = 0.06 s. The Gaussian
sigma is one quarter of that width, 0.015 s. The default stratum minimum
is 200 spikes, and a stratum still needs a run longer than 0.5 s. Models
3-6 and 9-15 use the strata saved in the summary, so a change of that
minimum is used only after one full run.


Model1 session
--------------
One row per neuron. The outcome is the mean LFP delta power of the runs
in the all-runs autocorrelogram. A session factor absorbs the power level
shared by the neurons in one recording.

  P_i = a
      + b1 FiringRate_i
      + b2 Burst_i
      + b3 FirstPeak_i
      + b4 SecondCycle_i
      + b5 ACGDelta_i
      + session of neuron i
      + error_i

Burst is the 0-50 ms autocorrelogram mass. FirstPeak and SecondCycle are
the two delta-cycle prominences. ACGDelta is the delta-band power of the
half-mirrored autocorrelogram after 100 ms.


Model2 beyond rate
-------------------
Same rows, outcome, and session factor as Model1. Every predictor except
firing rate is first replaced by its residual on firing rate. Firing rate
then keeps the variance it shares with the other predictors, and the other
coefficients are the association beyond firing rate.

  Z_i = X_i - projection of X_i on FiringRate_i

  P_i = a
      + b1 FiringRate_i
      + b2 Z(Burst)_i
      + b3 Z(FirstPeak)_i
      + b4 Z(SecondCycle)_i
      + b5 Z(ACGDelta)_i
      + session of neuron i
      + error_i


Model3 strata
-------------
One row per power stratum. Runs of a neuron are ordered from low to high
LFP delta power and split into strata of about equal spike count. The
neuron is a fixed factor, so each neuron has its own intercept. Ordinary
least squares gives every stratum the same weight, and the usual p-value
treats strata as independent. A neuron with more strata therefore pulls
the slope more, and the p-value is smaller than a neuron-level test.

  P_ij = a_i
       + b1 FiringRate_ij
       + b2 Burst_ij
       + b3 FirstPeak_ij
       + b4 SecondCycle_ij
       + b5 ACGDelta_ij
       + error_ij

Only a neuron with at least two usable strata enters. The same fit is
printed for all neurons and, in the same file, for each lock group.


Model4 strata residual
----------------------
Same rows and neuron intercepts as Model3. Neuron identity is removed
first: every predictor is replaced by its deviation from its own neuron
mean. Every predictor except firing rate is then replaced by its residual
on that within-neuron firing rate. The firing-rate coefficient is the
within-neuron slope. The other coefficients are within-neuron associations
beyond firing rate. The p-values are still ordinary least-squares p-values.

  X_ij = predictor_ij - mean over strata of neuron i
  Z_ij = X_ij - projection of X_ij on the within-neuron firing rate

Model2 does not do this. It has one row per neuron, so neuron identity is
not a repeated factor there.


Model5 sequential
-----------------
Same rows and outcome as Model3. Predictors are orthogonalized in this
order:

  1. Neuron
  2. Firing rate
  3. Burst, 0-50 ms
  4. ACG delta power, >100 ms
  5. First-peak prominence
  6. Second-cycle prominence

Neuron identity is first. At each step, every later predictor is replaced
by its residual on the current one. Firing rate therefore keeps only the
within-neuron variance, and every predictor after firing rate is orthogonal
to both the neuron and that within-neuron firing rate. The printed added
R^2 is the variance that step explains after the earlier steps.


Model6 entrainment
------------------
Two outcomes, one statistics file. Neuron is the fixed factor. The
printed number is the partial R^2 of LFP delta power.

Folded autocorrelogram delta power, with firing rate as the covariate:

  ACGDelta_ij = a_i
              + bP LFPDeltaPower_ij
              + bR FiringRate_ij
              + error_ij

First-peak prominence, with firing rate and burst as the covariates:

  FirstPeak_ij = c_i
               + dP LFPDeltaPower_ij
               + dR FiringRate_ij
               + dB Burst_ij
               + error_ij


Model7 PPC session
------------------
One row per neuron. The outcome is mean PPC of the runs in the all-runs
autocorrelogram. LFP delta power is a predictor here. The session factor
is the same kind of factor as in Model1.

  PPC_i = a
        + b1 FiringRate_i
        + b2 LFPDeltaPower_i
        + b3 Burst_i
        + b4 FirstPeak_i
        + b5 SecondCycle_i
        + b6 ACGDelta_i
        + session of neuron i
        + error_i


Model8 PPC residual
-------------------
Same rows and outcome as Model7. Every predictor except firing rate,
including LFP delta power, is replaced by its residual on firing rate.
The rate-free coefficients are the association with PPC beyond firing rate.


Model9 PPC strata
-----------------
Same stratum rows and neuron intercepts as Model3. The outcome is the
PPC of the stratum, and LFP delta power is added to the predictors.

  PPC_ij = a_i
         + b1 FiringRate_ij
         + b2 Burst_ij
         + b3 FirstPeak_ij
         + b4 SecondCycle_ij
         + b5 ACGDelta_ij
         + b6 LFPDeltaPower_ij
         + error_ij

A full run has to store stratum PPC before this model can be fit.


Model10 PPC strata residual
---------------------------
Same neuron-first step as Model4, with stratum PPC as the outcome. Neuron
identity is removed first. Every predictor except firing rate, including
LFP delta power, is then residualized on the within-neuron firing rate.
Model8 does not do the neuron step: it has one row per neuron.


Model11 PPC sequential
----------------------
Same rows and outcome as Model9. Orthogonalization order:

  1. LFP delta power
  2. Neuron
  3. Firing rate
  4. Burst, 0-50 ms
  5. ACG delta power, >100 ms
  6. First-peak prominence
  7. Second-cycle prominence

LFP delta power is first. Neuron identity is second, so firing rate and
every later predictor keep only within-neuron variance that power does
not explain.


Model12 run rate
----------------
One row per firing run, not per stratum. Neuron identity supplies an
intercept. Neuron type supplies its own intercept and its own slope.
The types are trough-locked, peak-locked, and non-entrained.

  P_ij = a_i
       + c_g(i)
       + b_g(i) (FiringRate_ij - mean firing rate)
       + error_ij

g(i) is the type of neuron i. The tests ask whether the slopes differ by
type and whether the intercepts differ by type. These rows are stored only
by a full run.


Model13 equal neuron
--------------------
This is the primary test of the oscillatory predictors. The rows are the
power strata. Each neuron's mean is subtracted from the outcome and from
every predictor, so the slope is within the neuron. There is no extra
random intercept: the neuron mean is already removed, and it is not put
back as a second term.

The weight of a stratum is 1 over the number of strata of its neuron.
Every neuron has total weight 1, whether it has two strata or many.

  P_ij - mean_i(P) =
        b1 (FiringRate_ij - mean_i(FiringRate))
      + b2 (Burst_ij - mean_i(Burst))
      + b3 (FirstPeak_ij - mean_i(FirstPeak))
      + b4 (SecondCycle_ij - mean_i(SecondCycle))
      + b5 (ACGDelta_ij - mean_i(ACGDelta))
      + error_ij

The fit is weighted least squares. The printed coefficients are
standardized on these within-neuron values. The standard error is
cluster-robust by neuron (CR1, multiplied by G/(G-1), with t and F
degrees of freedom equal to the number of neurons minus the number of
coefficients). Strata of one neuron are not treated as independent
observations.

The joint test is

  b3 = b4 = b5 = 0

Firing rate and burst stay in the model, so a significant joint test is
not the firing-rate association and not the 0-50 ms burst. The same file
contains the all-neuron fit and a separate copy for each lock group:
trough-locked, peak-locked, delta-locked, and non-entrained. Model15 is
the comparison of those slopes inside one equation.


Model14 row weight
------------------
The same equation, the same predictors, and the same cluster-robust joint
test as Model13. The only change is the weight:

  weight_ij = 1

Every stratum has an equal vote, so a neuron with many strata pulls the
slope more than a neuron with two. The difference between the Model13
and Model14 coefficients is the pull of those many-strata neurons. This
is the check beside Model13, not a second primary result. It is fit for
all neurons and again inside each lock group, the same divisions as
Model13. Model15 is the model that puts cell type in the equation, so
that one is not copied by group.


Model15 group slopes
--------------------
The same within-neuron demeaning, the same equal-neuron weights, and the
same cluster-robust standard errors as Model13. Each of the five
predictors has its own slope in each neuron type. The types are
trough-locked, peak-locked, and non-entrained. Delta-locked neurons are
not included.

  P_ij - mean_i(P) =
      sum over predictors X and types g of
      b_{X,g} (X_ij - mean_i(X)) * (neuron i is type g)
      + error_ij

A separate type intercept is not identified, because subtracting the
neuron mean already removes it. Firing rate and burst have type-specific
slopes as well, so a type difference in the firing-rate slope is not
read as a difference in an oscillatory slope.

The joint test is that the three oscillatory slopes do not differ by type:

  b_FirstPeak,trough     = b_FirstPeak,peak     = b_FirstPeak,nonentrained
  b_SecondCycle,trough   = b_SecondCycle,peak   = b_SecondCycle,nonentrained
  b_ACGDelta,trough      = b_ACGDelta,peak      = b_ACGDelta,nonentrained

The individual lines in the statistics file are each slope against zero.
The figure shows the type-specific slopes and the joint type test.


Per-neuron slopes
-----------------
Separate from Model15. A neuron enters only when it has at least
min_strata_per_neuron finite strata. The default is 10. For that neuron,
LFP delta power is the predictor and each autocorrelogram measure is its
own outcome: ACG delta power, first-peak prominence, and second-cycle
prominence.

The slope is ordinary least squares after strata with a modified z-score
above 3.5, on LFP power or on the outcome, are removed. It is reported
only when at least 6 strata remain. Its p-value is the usual t-test.
Spearman rank correlation uses every finite stratum and does not drop
those points.

Each outcome is summarized for all neurons and for each lock group:
a histogram of the slopes, a violin with one point per neuron, the count
of significantly positive and significantly negative slopes, and a
Wilcoxon signed-rank test of the slopes against zero. The violin and the
signed-rank test are repeated for the rank correlations.
